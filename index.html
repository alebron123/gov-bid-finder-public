Why this is defensible

Federal contract data is free, so nobody can build a business on it alone. The moat is the local data: Miami-Dade County, the City of Miami, the school district, the water and sewer department. Each has its own portal and no bulk download, which is exactly why nobody aggregates them. It is tedious, and tedious is what automation is for.

The second moat is the renewal calendar. Knowing a contract expires in eight months is worth more than knowing one closes on Friday, because eight months is enough time to prepare. Nobody sells that to small contractors.

Who we are competing with

GovWin from Deltek costs about $29,000 a year and serves roughly 5,800 customers. GovSpend runs $7,300 to $24,500. GovTribe starts at $1,350. All of them are built for companies that already bid and have someone whose job is bidding.

In three months of award data, more than ten thousand different companies won federal work, and the median one took about $500,000. Hundreds of thousands more are registered and never win anything. Nobody serves them, because a $29,000 product cannot be sold to a roofer. That gap is the business.

What this is

Every open US federal contract opportunity, plus State of Florida solicitations, in one searchable place. The federal data comes from the SAM.gov daily public extract; the Florida data comes from MyFloridaMarketPlace.

Why it exists

Governments buy ordinary things constantly: painting, landscaping, janitorial, IT support, catering, translation. Most small businesses never bid, because each notice is a wall of paperwork. Contracts under $100,000 routinely draw only a handful of bidders.
